Case Study A: “Say Your Name Again?”

Theme: Microaggression

Format: 4-voice — spoken dialogue + inner monologue. Assign four readers or two readers doubling inner voices.

TOM — white man, 58, longtime member

SORAYA — Iranian-American woman, 37, first-time visitor

Scene

TOM (spoken, genial): I don’t think we’ve met! I’m Tom.
TOM (inner): New faces brighten coffee hour — be welcoming, Tom.
SORAYA (spoken, warm): Lovely to meet you, Tom. I’m Soraya Rezai.
SORAYA (inner): He’s about to repeat it — happens a lot.
TOM (spoken, leaning in): So‑RYE‑ah. Wow — what a gorgeous name! Could you… say it again? I just love the sound.
TOM (inner): Nailed the pronunciation. She’ll feel seen.
SORAYA (spoken, polite): Soraya.
SORAYA (inner): Performative fascination — here we go.
TOM (spoken): Beautiful. You must get compliments like that all the time.
TOM (inner): Build rapport — she’ll stay.
SORAYA (spoken): Sometimes. I was born in Maryland, though — my parents loved Persian poetry.
SORAYA (inner): Offer context, redirect.
TOM (spoken, animated): Fascinating! Our multicultural service is next month — you could share that story from the pulpit. Maybe teach us a phrase in… Farsi, right?
TOM (inner): Great idea — celebrate diversity!
SORAYA (spoken, measured): I’ll think about it. Today I’m just hoping to find community.
SORAYA (inner): Feeling spotlighted, not welcomed.
TOM (spoken): Absolutely! Let me grab a visitor card.
TOM (inner): Mission accomplished.

Discussion Prompts

  • What do you notice about how this conversation unfolds?
  • What assumptions are being made by each character?
  • How is each person experiencing this interaction differently?
  • What might be the impact of Tom’s comments about Soraya’s name and background?
  • Have you seen or experienced moments like this in your congregation?

After Discussion

  • Whole-group harvest: themes recorded
  • Facilitator analysis made and recorded
  • Each person writes at least one personal takeaway
