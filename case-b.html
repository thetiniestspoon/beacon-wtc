Case Study B: “Resource Rescue”

Theme: Savior Complex

Format: 4-voice — spoken dialogue + inner monologue. Assign four readers or two readers doubling inner voices.

MOLLY — white woman, 46, chaplain

DARIUS — Black man, 35, congregant

Scene

MOLLY (spoken, upbeat on phone): Darius! I couldn’t stop thinking about your job search and put together a surprise plan.
MOLLY (inner): He’ll be so grateful I leapt into action.
DARIUS (spoken, guarded): Oh? Thanks for calling.
DARIUS (inner): I asked for prayer, not a project manager…
MOLLY (spoken): Good news first: my friend at CareerWorks squeezed you into a résumé clinic Thursday at 9 a.m.!
MOLLY (inner): Networks are power — share them!
DARIUS (spoken): Thursday morning — I have the kids.
DARIUS (inner): No childcare, no discussion.
MOLLY (spoken, rapid): Already covered! Tess from church offered to babysit. She’ll arrive 8:30.
MOLLY (inner): See? One step ahead.
DARIUS (spoken, slower): Molly, that’s generous, yet it feels a bit fast. I’m trying to process everything.
DARIUS (inner): Control is slipping from my hands.
MOLLY (spoken, stumbling): Oh — goodness, I — I’m so sorry! I just wanted to help. I probably overstepped. I feel awful now.
MOLLY (inner): Ugh, Molly, you messed up. He must think you’re a meddler.
DARIUS (spoken, gentle): It’s okay. Maybe we can start with a conversation about what support looks like for me?
DARIUS (inner): Can she stay with my feelings, not hers?
MOLLY (spoken, voice quivers): Yes, of course. I’m really embarrassed — I hate that I made this about me.
MOLLY (inner): Center him, Molly. Deep breath.
DARIUS (spoken): How about we pray together, then brainstorm next steps when I’m ready?
DARIUS (inner): Partnership, not rescue.
MOLLY (spoken, calmer): Absolutely. I’m listening.
MOLLY (inner): Step back, hold space.

Reflection Questions

  1. Intention: Molly is clearly trying to help. At what point does her helping become something else? What is she getting out of the “surprise plan”?
  2. Tension: Darius says, “It feels a bit fast.” What is he really saying — and what does it cost him to say it?
  3. Attention: When Molly realizes she’s overstepped, she says “I feel awful” and “I’m really embarrassed.” Where does her attention go in that moment — toward Darius, or toward herself?
  4. Retention: Darius asks for prayer and partnership. Molly offered a résumé clinic, a babysitter, and an apology about her own feelings. What will Darius take away from this interaction about what “help” means in this community?
  5. Darius asked for prayer. Molly delivered a project plan. What does this gap reveal about how we define care — and whose definition wins?
  6. Have you seen or experienced moments like this in your congregation? What makes it hard to slow down when someone else is in need?

Facilitator Guidance

Running a 20-Minute Group Discussion

Minutes 1–5: Read Together

Assign four readers — one for Molly’s spoken lines, one for Molly’s inner monologue, one for Darius’s spoken lines, and one for Darius’s inner monologue. Read the full scenario aloud. Ask the group to pay attention to the pace of the conversation — who is driving it, and who is trying to slow it down.

Minutes 5–12: Individual Responses

Go around the table. Each person selects one reflection question and shares their response. Encourage people to name the specific moment they felt the dynamic shift. The facilitator or a recorder captures key phrases on a shared surface.

Minutes 12–20: Guided Synthesis

Name the pattern: Molly acts, Darius reacts, Molly centers her own feelings about the reaction. Ask: “What would it look like for Molly to truly follow Darius’s lead?” Explore the difference between doing for and being with. Close by asking: “When has your desire to help gotten ahead of someone’s actual need?”

Sample Answers

Intention: Molly wants to alleviate Darius’s struggle. Her inner monologue reveals she also wants to feel effective and generous — “He’ll be so grateful I leapt into action.” The surprise plan is as much about Molly’s self-image as a helper as it is about Darius’s needs. She’s not asking what he wants; she’s delivering what she’d want.

Tension: “It feels a bit fast” is an understatement. Darius’s inner monologue says “control is slipping from my hands.” He has to manage both his own situation and Molly’s feelings about being told to slow down. That’s a double burden — and it’s one that often falls along racial lines, where Black congregants end up caretaking the emotions of white helpers.

Attention: The moment Molly realizes she’s overstepped, her attention swings entirely to her own shame: “I feel awful,” “I’m really embarrassed,” “I hate that I made this about me.” Ironically, saying “I made this about me” is still making it about her. Darius has to reassure Molly before he can even begin to articulate what support actually looks like for him.

Retention: Darius may take away that help in this community comes with strings — that accepting care means accepting someone else’s plan for your life. He may be slower to share vulnerability next time, not because people don’t care, but because their care felt like control.

The gap between prayer and project plan is the heart of this scenario. Darius asked for spiritual companionship; Molly heard a problem to solve. This reveals a care model that values action over presence — and assumes that the helper knows what’s needed better than the person living it.

It’s hard to slow down because doing something feels better than sitting with discomfort. Molly’s networks and resourcefulness are real assets — but they become liabilities when deployed without consent. The chaplain’s first tool is not a referral; it’s a question.

More Ideal Choices

What Molly Did What Molly Could Do Instead
Put together a “surprise plan” without asking “I’ve been thinking about you. Can I ask what kind of support would be most helpful right now?”
Booked a résumé clinic and arranged childcare “I know some people who might be helpful when you’re ready. Want me to hold onto that until you say the word?”
Centered her own shame: “I feel awful” “Thank you for telling me. I hear you — let’s start where you are.”
“I hate that I made this about me” (Process that feeling privately; in this moment, stay with Darius)

UU Principles in Play

Inherent worth and dignity: Darius’s dignity includes his right to define what help looks like. Agency is not a luxury — it’s a condition of genuine care.

Justice and equity: The savior dynamic often tracks along lines of race and class. Molly’s access to networks and resources doesn’t make her the expert on Darius’s life — but structural power can make it feel that way to both of them.

Interdependence: True interdependence means mutual vulnerability. Molly could learn from Darius about what faithful presence looks like — if she can stop long enough to listen.

Accountability (8th Principle): When white helpers leap to action for Black congregants without asking, it echoes a long history of paternalism. Accountability means examining not just whether we help, but how — and who gets to decide.
